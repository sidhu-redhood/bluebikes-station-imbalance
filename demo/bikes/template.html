    How the model works

    Each station’s state is its bike count, 0 to capacity. Within each clock hour, riders return bikes at one rate and take them at another, both estimated from trip history. That makes the count a birth–death Markov chain: up one when a bike comes back, down one when a bike leaves, with returns blocked at full and pickups blocked at empty.

    The tracker reads each station every 10 minutes, as the planned GBFS polling would, and turns the hourly rates into a 10-minute transition matrix P = exp(Q·10). Multiplying the current state through those matrices gives the forecast. Making “empty” an absorbing state gives the chance the station runs out at any moment before a later poll, not only at a poll.

    The day itself is simulated rider by rider from the same rates, so the forecasts here are scored in the best case. The real test is scoring them against your own GBFS polls.
